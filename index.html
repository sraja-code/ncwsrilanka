About Us

Our History

How the National Commission on Women came to be established, and what it now oversees.

The National Commission on Women was established under the Women Empowerment Act No. 37 of 2024, certified on 2 July 2024 and published in the Government Gazette.

The Commission is the result of decades of work by women's rights groups and organisations across Sri Lanka, who campaigned for an independent national body dedicated to protecting and advancing women's rights.

Seven Commissioners were appointed by H.E. the President Anura Kumara Dissanayake in September 2025, based on the recommendations of the Constitutional Council and the Women Parliamentarians' Caucus.

As an independent body, the Commission oversees the Ministry of Women and Child Affairs and monitors how the National Policy on Gender Equality and Women's Empowerment is being carried out.

Key Dates

  • Sep 2025Seven Commissioners appointed by H.E. the President
  • OngoingCommission begins operations and regional outreach

Women Empowerment Act No. 37 of 2024

The law that establishes the National Commission on Women and sets out its powers and duties. Certified on 2 July 2024.

This is the law that created the National Commission on Women. It came into effect in July 2024.

  • Every woman in Sri Lanka has rights that cannot be taken away from her.
  • A commission of seven independent people has been set up to protect those rights.
  • You can make a complaint to the NCW if your rights have been violated, free of charge and without a lawyer.
  • The NCW must respond to your complaint within 14 days.
  • If your case goes to mediation, it must be resolved within 60 days.
  • If you are not happy with the decision, you can appeal to the Court of Appeal within one month.
  • The NCW can also investigate problems on its own, without a complaint being made first.
Section 4 · Objectives of the Act

What the Act Sets Out to Achieve

01

Introduce ways to protect women's rights under the Constitution

02

Carry out Sri Lanka's obligations under CEDAW and related laws

03

Create a national policy for the advancement and empowerment of women

04

Protect women from discrimination based on gender and sexual orientation

05

Prevent harassment, marginalisation, sexual harassment and violence against women

06

Recommend law reforms to stop discrimination and support women's empowerment

07

Support women's participation in justice and equal representation in decision-making

08

Ensure women's economic empowerment, with special attention to women with disabilities

09

Run public education programmes on all aspects of women's empowerment

10

Promote equal pay for equal work

Section 6 · Powers of the Commission

What the NCW Can Do

Investigate Violations

Look into rights violations, on its own initiative or when a complaint is received.

Go to Court

Intervene in court cases about women's rights and take action in any competent court.

Mediation

Send cases to a trained mediation panel, either on complaint or on its own motion.

Monitor Compliance

Check that private bodies and government institutions are upholding women's rights.

Policy Formulation

Develop the National Policy on women's advancement and empowerment.

Recommend Law Changes

Advise the Government on updating laws to meet international standards.

Regional Offices

Open offices in provinces and regions across Sri Lanka for easier access.

Award Compensation

Award compensation to complainants for reasonable costs incurred in making a complaint.

Part II · Inquiry & Enforcement

Key Timelines

OwnMotion

Own-Motion Investigations

May be started without a complaint. Must conclude within one month (Section 18).

14Days

Response to Complaints

The NCW must inform the complainant of the action taken within 14 days (Section 19).

60Days

Mediation

Mediation must be completed within 60 days of referral (Section 20).

1Month

Right of Appeal

You may appeal to the Court of Appeal within one month (Section 26).

SCReferral

Supreme Court References

The Commission receives references from the Supreme Court on women's rights matters under Article 126, and may refer questions on the scope of women's rights to the Supreme Court during an inquiry (Section 17).

Members of the Commission

Seven Commissioners were appointed in September 2025 by H.E. the President.

Five Commissioners were appointed on the recommendation of the Constitutional Council, and two on the recommendation of the Women Parliamentarians' Caucus.

At least five members, including the Chairperson, must be women. Commissioners serve four-year terms and must have expertise in law, trade unionism, management, health, education or women's empowerment.

About Us

Organisational Structure

How the Commission is organised under the Women Empowerment Act No. 37 of 2024.

The Commission

A Chairperson and seven Commissioners, appointed for four-year terms, set the direction of the NCW and make decisions on inquiries, mediation and policy.

The Secretariat

Supports the Commission's day-to-day work, including administration, finance and case management.

Divisions

The Secretariat is organised into operational divisions covering the Commission's core functions. Divisions will be listed here once confirmed.

Regional and Provincial Offices

The Act empowers the Commission to open offices across Sri Lanka's provinces and regions, for easier access outside Colombo.

The Ombuds

An independent office that can receive complaints about gender-based violence or rights violations and forward recommendations to the Commission.

National Fund for Women

Managed by the Commission, the Fund receives money from Parliament and can accept donations from local and international sources.
