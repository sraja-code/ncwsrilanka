About Us

Our History

How the National Commission on Women came to be established, and what it now oversees.

The National Commission on Women was established under the Women Empowerment Act No. 37 of 2024, certified on 2 July 2024 and published in the Government Gazette.

The Commission is the result of decades of work by women's rights groups and organisations across Sri Lanka, who campaigned for a national body dedicated to protecting and advancing women's rights.

The first Commissioners were appointed by H.E. the President Anura Kumara Dissanayake in September 2025, based on the recommendations of the Constitutional Council and the Women Parliamentarians' Caucus.

The Commission has oversight powers in relation to all Government Ministries, and a mandate to monitor how the National Policy on Gender Equality and Women's Empowerment is being implemented.

Women Empowerment Act No. 37 of 2024

The law that establishes the National Commission on Women and sets out its powers and duties. Certified on 2 July 2024.

📄 View in English 📄 සිංහල 📄 தமிழ்

This is the law that created the National Commission on Women. It came into effect in July 2024.

  • Every woman in Sri Lanka has rights that cannot be taken away from her.
  • A commission of seven independent people has been set up to protect those rights.
  • If your rights have been violated, or are about to be, you or someone acting for you can apply to the NCW within one month.
  • Once the NCW decides on your complaint, it must tell you what action it has taken within 14 days.
  • If your case is sent to mediation, the mediation must be concluded within 60 days.
  • If you are not satisfied with the NCW's decision, you can appeal to the Court of Appeal within one month.
  • The NCW can also investigate problems on its own, without a complaint being made first.
Section 2 · Objectives of the Act

What the Act Sets Out to Achieve

01

Introduce ways to protect women's rights under the Constitution

02

Carry out Sri Lanka's obligations under CEDAW and related laws

03

Formulate and implement the national policy for the advancement and empowerment of women

04

Protect women from discrimination based on gender and sexual orientation

05

Prevent discrimination, marginalisation, sexual harassment and violence against women

06

Recommend law reforms to stop discrimination and support women's empowerment

07

Support women's participation in justice and equal representation in decision-making

08

Ensure women's economic empowerment, with special attention to women with disabilities

09

Run public education programmes on all aspects of women's empowerment

10

Promote equal pay for men and women in employment of equal value

Section 6 · Powers of the Commission

What the NCW Can Do

Investigate Violations

Look into rights violations, on its own initiative or when a complaint is received.

Go to Court

Intervene in court cases about women's rights and take action in any competent court.

Mediation

Refer cases of discrimination to the Panel of Mediators, either on a complaint or on its own motion.

Monitor Compliance

Monitor how private persons and non-state institutions follow policies and practices relating to women's rights.

Policy Formulation

Develop the National Policy on women's advancement and empowerment.

Recommend Law Changes

Advise the Government on updating laws to meet international standards.

Regional Offices

Open offices in provinces and regions across Sri Lanka for easier access.

Complaint Expenses

Award an aggrieved person a sum to meet the expenses reasonably incurred in making a complaint.

Part II · Inquiry & Enforcement

Key Timelines

1Month

Making a Complaint

An application must be made within one month of the infringement, or of becoming aware of it (Section 19).

OwnMotion

Inquiry and Investigation

The Commission may act on a complaint or on its own motion. A Committee appointed by the Commission conducts the inquiry within one month of receiving the application or information (Section 18).

14Days

Informing the Aggrieved Person

After deciding on a complaint, the Commission must inform the aggrieved person of the action taken within 14 days of the decision (Section 21).

60Days

Mediation

The Panel of Mediators must conclude mediation within 60 days of the referral (Section 22).

1Month

Right of Appeal

A person aggrieved by a decision of the Commission may appeal to the Court of Appeal within one month of being informed of it (Section 26).

SCReferral

Supreme Court References

The Commission receives references from the Supreme Court on women's rights matters under Article 126, and may refer questions on the scope of women's rights to the Supreme Court during an inquiry (Section 17).

Members of the Commission

The Commission has seven members, including the Chairperson, appointed by H.E. the President.

Five members are appointed on the recommendation of the Constitutional Council, and two on the recommendation of the Women Parliamentarians' Caucus.

At least five members, including the Chairperson, must be women. Members hold office for four years and must have proven knowledge and experience in fields such as law, trade unionism, management, economic development, health, education or women's empowerment.

Organisational Structure

How the Commission is organised under the Women Empowerment Act No. 37 of 2024.

The Commission

Seven members, including the Chairperson, appointed for a term of four years. The Commission sets the direction of the NCW and makes decisions on inquiries, mediation and policy.

The Secretariat

Headed by the Executive Director, the Secretariat supports the Commission's day-to-day work.

Divisions

The Secretariat is organised into four divisions: Administration, Finance, Research and Development, and Legal Affairs. View the Divisions.

Regional and Provincial Offices

The Act empowers the Commission to open offices across Sri Lanka's provinces and regions, for easier access outside Colombo.

The Ombuds for Women's Rights

An office provided for under the Act that can receive complaints about gender-based violence or rights violations and forward recommendations to the Commission.

National Fund for Women

Managed by the Commission, the Fund receives money from Parliament and can accept donations from local and international sources.

Strategic Plan 2027–2031

The Commission's first five-year Strategic Plan sets out its vision, mission and priorities for 2027 to 2031. It was developed with all seven Commissioners and the Executive Director, and through consultations with women's rights organisations and other stakeholders.

📄 View the full Plan (English) Sinhala and Tamil versions forthcoming

Our Vision

All women in Sri Lanka enjoy their right to equality, dignity and justice.

Our Mission

To protect and promote women's rights and gender equality through independent oversight, advocacy, and strengthening inclusive participation and accountability.

Thematic Areas

The Plan is organised around three thematic areas.

1. Access to Justice

To promote gender responsive laws and policies relating to access to justice, equal participation and representation.

  • Establish the Commission's own investigation, inquiry and mediation processes, and a regional presence.
  • Analyse existing laws and policies for compliance with CEDAW, and intervene in ongoing law reform processes.
  • Monitor the implementation of the National Policy on Gender Equality and Women's Empowerment 2023–2033.
  • Promote sex-disaggregated data and women's representation in decision-making bodies.

2. Safety, Peace and Security

Gender-responsive access to justice is ensured in relation to safety, peace and security.

  • Sexual and Gender-Based Violence: monitor the Multisectoral National Action Plan to Address Sexual and Gender-Based Violence 2024–2028, address sexual harassment including at the workplace under ILO Convention 190, and undertake an islandwide study on rape.
  • Women, Peace and Security: monitor the National Action Plan on Women, Peace and Security 2023–2027, and advocate for women affected by conflict.

3. Access to Resources and Opportunities

To promote gender responsive laws, policies and practices in the workplace, access to gender responsive health services that cover all phases of a woman's life, and gender-responsive actions to climate change and disasters.

  • Economic Justice: provide a gendered perspective on labour law reform and advocate for recognition of women's contribution to the economy.
  • Women's Access to Health: promote free and quality healthcare for women throughout their lifespan.
  • Environment, Climate Change and Disasters: address women's representation in climate and disaster decision-making, and advocate for sex-disaggregated data.

How the Commission Will Work

Six strategic interventions cut across all three thematic areas.

Policy and Law Reform Inquiry, Litigation and Mediation Referrals and Collaboration Monitoring Research and Fact Finding Advocacy

National Fund for Women

The Commission will set up and manage the National Fund for Women, as provided for in the Women Empowerment Act, and will establish a Fund Management Committee as an oversight mechanism.

Institutional Development

As a new Commission, it will focus on governance, infrastructure, financial management, human resources, communications, outreach and public engagement, including outreach across all nine provinces.
